# Outreach exports

CSV batches of hotels for contact-finding (Apollo and similar). Generated by
`scripts/export-outreach-batch.mjs` (ordered by posting activity) and
`scripts/export-outreach-low-er.mjs` (ordered by lowest engagement rate). Both
are **read-only** against Supabase and touch no breakout, baseline or
engagement logic in the app.

## Batches

| File | Scope | Hotels | Generated |
|---|---|---|---|
| `apollo-batch-01-uk-ireland.csv` | independently-run tracked hotels in the UK + Ireland | 10 | 2026-07-30 |
| `apollo-batch-01-uk-ireland-deferred-groups.csv` | the group-run UK + Ireland properties, held back | 11 | 2026-07-30 |
| `apollo-batch-02-europe-low-er.csv` | tracked hotels in 11 UK/Western-European countries, lowest median ER first | 50 | 2026-07-30 |
| `apollo-batch-03-europe-independent-low-er.csv` | **independently-run only**, all of `region = Europe`, UK block first then the rest, lowest median ER inside each | 42 | 2026-07-30 |

## Regenerating / new batches

```bash
node scripts/export-outreach-batch.mjs
```

```bash
node scripts/export-outreach-low-er.mjs
```

```bash
node scripts/export-outreach-batch.mjs --countries "France,Italy" --limit 30 --out outreach/apollo-batch-02-france-italy.csv
```

Group-run properties are excluded by default. To include them in the main file
and skip the deferred split:

```bash
node scripts/export-outreach-batch.mjs --include-groups
```

## Selection rules

- `hotels.tracked = true`
- has at least one row in `posts` (matched on `instagram_handle`)
- has a non-blank `hotels.website`
- `hotels.country` matches the requested list
- **independently run** (see below), unless `--include-groups` is passed
- ordered by posts in the last 90 days, then by follower count

## Independent vs group

Decided off the website URL, not off brand knowledge. A hotel is treated as
**group-run** when either signal fires:

1. its root domain is shared with another hotel in the `hotels` table
2. its website is a deep path under a brand site (`/en/hotels/claridges`)

Either way a contact-finding tool returns the group's head office rather than the
property's own social lead, so a cold email lands in the wrong inbox. On the
UK + Ireland set the two signals agree exactly — **10 independent, 11 group**, no
hotel triggers one signal without the other.

The `ownership` and `hotels_sharing_domain` columns carry this through to the CSV,
so the call is auditable rather than taken on trust.

Held back in batch 01: Claridge's, The Connaught, The Berkeley (`maybourne.com`,
4 properties); both Four Seasons (`fourseasons.com`, 44); The Dorchester, Coworth
Park (`dorchestercollection.com`, 9); Mandarin Oriental Hyde Park
(`mandarinoriental.com`, 27); Rosewood London (`rosewoodhotels.com`, 15); Raffles
London at The OWO (`raffles.com`, 6); Shangri-La The Shard (`shangri-la.com`, 4).
Reach these via the property's social lead on LinkedIn, not a domain lookup.

**The Savoy is classified independent** and it is worth knowing why: it sits on its
own `thesavoylondon.com` root, unshared, so both signals pass. It is Accor-managed,
but the domain test is asking "will a domain lookup reach this hotel's own team?" —
and there, its own site means yes.

## Batch 02 — lowest engagement rate

Same table, a different question: *which hotels have an audience but aren't
reaching it?* Ordered by **lowest median per-post ER**, ascending.

ER here mirrors the leaderboard's definition in `lib/data.ts`: the median of
`(likes + comments) / followers` over the hotel's most recent 30 posts **with a
visible like count**, within the last 90 days. `er_median` is written as a
**percentage to 3 dp** — `0.093` means 0.093% of followers engage with a median
post, not 9.3%.

Selection: `tracked = true`, non-blank website, ≥30 followers, ≥5 posts in 90
days, and **≥5 posts with visible likes** — the last of which is doing the real
work (see below). 68 tracked hotels are in scope across the 11 countries, 63
qualify, and the batch is the lowest 50 of those. The floors on followers and
post count are non-binding at this scale; every hotel in scope clears them.

### ⚠ The `likes_count = 3` sentinel

Instagram hides likes on a growing share of posts, and the scraper writes
**three** different sentinels for that — but only two are documented in
`CLAUDE.md`:

| value | rows | documented? |
|---|---|---|
| `null` | 952 | yes |
| `-1` | 118 | yes |
| **`3`** | **813** | **no** |

Exactly 3 likes occurs 813 times, while 1 occurs 0 times, 2 twice and 4 three
times — a 200× spike at a single value, growing month over month (3 rows in Aug
2025, 316 in Jun 2026). 112 of those rows carry more than 20 comments; one has
468. No genuine 3-like post does that.

Counting them as real drags a hotel's median ER to near zero, so a "lowest ER"
sort returns **the hotels the scraper failed on**, not the hotels with an
engagement problem. Before excluding them this batch opened with Mandarin
Oriental Paris at 0.003% and Ashford Castle at 0.014%; after excluding them
Ashford Castle sits 49th at 0.427%. Across the 11-country scope, 361 of 1,974
posts in the window (18.3%) are hidden-like rows.

Five hotels are rejected outright for having too few visible-like posts to
measure — **Castiglion del Bosco** (0 of 41), **Shangri-La Paris** (0 of 32),
**Rosewood London** (3 of 26), **Hôtel du Couvent** (0 of 6) and **The Dolder
Grand** (2 of 10). These are *unmeasured*, not low-engagement; don't pitch them
on engagement.

**`hasVisibleLikes` in `lib/data.ts` filters `null` and `-1` but not `3`,** so
the product is still counting these rows in every ER, baseline and breakout
multiplier. That fix is deliberately not made in this batch — it changes
selection logic and belongs in its own change.

### Group-run properties dominate this batch

Batch 01 held group-run properties back, because a domain lookup returns the
group's head office rather than the property's social lead. Batch 02 **keeps
them in**, marked in the `ownership` column, because sorting by lowest ER
surfaces the big collections: **34 of the 50 are group-run, 16 independent**.
Six root domains repeat inside the file (`mandarinoriental.com` ×5,
`dorchestercollection.com` ×4, `fourseasons.com` ×4, `maybourne.com` ×3,
`raffles.com` ×2, `oetkerhotels.com` ×2) — Apollo will return the same company
for each. Filter on `ownership = independent` for the 16 that a domain lookup
will actually reach.

## Batch 03 — independent only, UK first, all of Europe

Batch 02 with the group properties stripped out and the net widened to the whole
`region = Europe` set. Ordered **UK block first, then the rest of Europe**, with
lowest median ER first inside each block. Same ER method and same hidden-likes
exclusion as batch 02.

```bash
node scripts/export-outreach-low-er.mjs --region Europe --independent-only \
  --include-untracked --uk-first --limit 50 \
  --out outreach/apollo-batch-03-europe-independent-low-er.csv
```

### It is 42 rows, not 50 — the data does not contain 50

This was asked for as 50 and the ceiling is lower. Nothing is padded to reach a
target. From 313 Europe hotels: 54 are group-run, 179 have **no follower
snapshot** (see below), 20 posted fewer than 5 times in 90 days, 17 have too few
visible-like posts to measure, 1 has no website. That leaves **42**.

To go higher, in order of how much they cost: flip promising untracked hotels to
`tracked = true` and let one scrape give them follower snapshots (the 179 bucket
is by far the biggest), widen past Europe, or drop the independent-only rule.

### `no follower snapshot` is not `small hotel`

`profile_snapshots` only covers tracked hotels, so **every untracked hotel has a
follower count of zero** — and ER needs a denominator. The 179 rejections in that
bucket are unmeasurable, not tiny; the list includes Firmdale's eight London
hotels, all eight Hospes properties, and most of the Greek and German long tail.
Flipping one to `tracked = true` and running a scrape gives it a snapshot and
makes it eligible. This is why the script reports that bucket separately rather
than folding it into `followers < 30`.

### `ownership` now has three values, not two

Batch 01 collapsed everything non-independent into `group`, which is too blunt
once you need volume. Batch 03 splits the middle out:

| value | meaning | rows | safe to bulk-upload? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `independent` | own unshared domain, root URL | 24 | yes |
| `independent-deep-path` | own unshared domain, but a sub-page URL | 18 | **eyeball first** |
| `group` | root domain shared with another hotel | — | excluded from this batch |

The middle band is genuinely mixed and the URL alone cannot separate it. Some are
the hotel's own domain merely linking an English page — `sirenuse.it/en/`,
`passalacqua.it/en/`, `sacher.com/en/vienna/`, `palace.ch/en/`, `reschio.com/hotel`,
`grandhotel.se/en`. Others are real brand domains that happen to have only one
property in our table — `beaumier.com` (Les Roches Rouges), `montecarlosbm.com`
(Hôtel de Paris Monte-Carlo), `lungarnocollection.com` (Portrait Firenze),
`panpacific.com` (Pan Pacific London), `hiddenhotels.com` (Gran Hotel Inglés).
Deciding between them needs brand knowledge, which this script deliberately does
not encode — hence the separate label rather than a silent call.

Cut the file at `ownership = independent` for the confident 24, or take all 42
and check those five domains by hand.

### 12 of the 42 are untracked

They carry `tracked = no` and are **not in the product** — real hotels with real
recent post data, but nothing to show them if they sign up until they are flipped
to `tracked = true` and scraped. They are: The Prince Akatoki London, The Egerton
House Hotel, The Chedi Andermatt, Gran Hotel Inglés, Grand Hotel Son Net,
Akelarre, Hassler Roma, Fairmont Hotel Vier Jahreszeiten, Grand Hôtel
(Stockholm), Gardena Grödnerhof, Portrait Firenze, Pan Pacific London.

## Country data is dirty

`hotels.country` stores the US two ways — `United States` (89) and `USA` (39).
UK/Ireland is clean, so batch 01 is unaffected, but any future US batch must
match both spellings or it will silently miss hotels.

`region` used to have the same problem — both `Asia-Pacific` and a legacy `Asia`
(plus a one-row `Oceania`). Fixed on 2026-07-30, see
`instagram-pipeline/setup-fold-asia-oceania.sql`; region is now one value per
destination, so an Asia batch can match `Asia-Pacific` alone.
